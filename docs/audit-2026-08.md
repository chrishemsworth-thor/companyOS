## 0. Executive summary

**What it is.** A genuinely substantial, genuinely working multi-tenant business
platform — double-entry finance, CRM, quotes, support, projects, HR/leave/claims —
on one Cloudflare Worker and one SQLite database, with 1,259 passing tests and a
live deployment. Four weeks of build produced ~87,000 lines across 69 merged PRs.
The engineering is not the problem.

**What it is worth.** The defensible asset is *one database, not five integrated
products*: an approved expense claim posts a dimensioned journal entry and moves
project margin in the same transaction. No assembled SME stack does that, and it is
already shipped. Secondary assets: a working autonomous collections agent with an
audit trail, and Malaysian HR localisation that is genuinely fiddly to copy.

**What is not true yet.** The marketing story is "agent-first". The build is
API-first with a strong human console. **2 of 50 event types reach an agent**;
there is no OpenAPI document, no MCP server, and one agent total. The repo's own
`docs/direction.md` says this plainly, which is to its credit.

**What blocks revenue.** Three things, in order:

1. **No SST and no MyInvois e-invoicing.** As of 1 July 2026 the LHDN mandate
   reaches businesses above RM500k turnover — which is every plausible customer.
   You cannot sell invoicing software in Malaysia in 2026 without it, and MyInvois
   is not mentioned anywhere in the repo.
2. **No way to charge anyone.** There is no billing, plan, seat or metering
   concept in the product. The `tenants` table has four columns.
3. **No payroll.** Payroll is *the* reason a Malaysian SME buys HR software, and
   the PRDs deliberately reject it. That closes off HR as a wedge.

**The honest read on difficulty.** Building this was the easy part. It competes
with an incumbent in five categories at once against installed bases of 600,000
(SQL) and 240,000 companies (AutoCount) sold through dealer networks, and the
product it wants to replace is the general ledger — the single highest-trust
purchase an SME makes. **Marketing 6/10, distribution 8/10, sales 8/10 in
difficulty.** The one genuine tailwind is that the e-invoicing mandate is forcing
every SME in the country to touch its invoicing stack this year, which is the best
migration window in a decade — and the product is currently unable to walk through it.

**Recommended posture:** stop adding modules. Close the compliance gap, pick the
collections wedge, sell to ten design partners you already know. Details in §8.

## 1. What is CompanyOS?

### 1.1 In one paragraph

CompanyOS is a multi-tenant business operating system that runs a whole company's
back office — money, customers, tickets, projects, people — as **one normalized
REST API over one shared database**, with AI agents intended as the primary
consumer and a human operator console as the secondary path. It runs as a single
Cloudflare Worker with a single D1 (SQLite) database per deployment: no wrapped
third-party apps, no VPS, no sync layer. Its thesis is that once every business
process lives in one schema, cross-domain questions that normally need an
integration project or a BI stack become plain SQL joins — and autonomous agents
become cheap to point at real work.

### 1.3 What actually works, module by module

**Finance — the reference department, and the strongest thing here.** A real
double-entry general ledger: balanced entries enforced *before* an atomic write,
append-only enforced by SQL triggers, corrections only by reversal, integer cents
throughout, a system chart of accounts seeded per tenant. Invoices carry line
items and a full lifecycle, and creating one posts Dr AR / Cr Revenue in the *same
atomic batch* as the invoice rows. Payments settle many-to-many. A daily cron
sweep flips past-due invoices and emits the event that drives the agent. Journal
lines carry five analytical dimensions (customer, project, department, employee,
cost centre), which is what makes project profitability a query rather than a
feature.

**The collections agent — the one piece of working autonomy.** A Durable Object,
one instance per tenant+customer, woken by overdue/payment events and a daily
`alarm()`. It assembles cross-module context (open invoices, payment history, CRM
activities, open deals, the resolved billing contact), asks an LLM for a
structured decision — risk score, remind/escalate/wait, channel, composed message
— and sends it. Every decision is Zod-validated and written to the append-only
event log as `collections.decision.v1`. On any LLM failure, or with no API key
configured, it falls back to a deterministic heuristic and template, so
collections never silently stops. Guardrails today: a 24-hour per-customer contact
cooldown, and payment receipt resets escalation state.

**Sales / CRM.** Customers, per-customer contacts with roles, leads with one-call
conversion to customer + contact + deal, a configurable deal pipeline, an
append-only activity log shared by humans and agents, derived customer health, and
a pluggable enrichment port (no real provider wired). Quote-to-cash is complete:
quotes with per-line discounts and header tax, branded server-rendered documents,
automatic expiry by cron, one-call conversion to invoice.

**Support.** Tickets with an explicit state machine — illegal transitions return
409 listing the legal ones — and append-only threaded messages. No agent.

**Build.** Projects and issues, plus signature-verified inbound webhooks from
JIRA, GitHub and Bitbucket that mirror external issues into Build idempotently and
log `code.push` / `code.pr_opened` / `code.pr_merged` onto the bus. Inbound only.

**People / HR — the deepest module after finance.** Directory, teams, reporting
lines with cycle detection. Leave: configurable types and entitlement policies
with tenure bands, three accrual methods, pro-rating for mid-year joiners,
carry-forward with caps and expiry, Malaysian public holidays *with state
variation* and per-tenant overrides, configurable work weeks per employee,
working-day cost preview before submission, manager approval through the shared
primitive, and balances that are **derived, never stored** — so a pending request
reduces the balance immediately and a rejection restores it with no compensating
write. Expense claims: multi-line with a mandatory receipt per line, per-category
limits that warn without blocking, mileage at a rate frozen at submission, and on
approval **a balanced journal entry stamped with employee, project and department**.

**Platform.** Multi-tenant from row one (`tenant_id` first in every composite PK).
Multi-company identity — email unique *per company*, so one person holds accounts
at several companies, logging in with workspace slug + email + password. Dual auth:
hashed per-tenant API keys for agents, PBKDF2 + HMAC-signed HttpOnly session
cookies + CSRF for humans, with the tenant API key never reaching the browser.
Capability enforcement on every `/v1` route via a single mount table, with a test
that walks the router and fails if any route ships ungated. Shared approvals,
notifications and file-storage primitives. Idempotency keys on money-touching
writes, cursor pagination, rate limiting, security headers, a July 2026 security
audit with fixes applied.

**Operator console.** A React/Vite SPA covering every module with full CRUD, an
approvals inbox with purpose-built cards per subject type, an agent-activity feed,
cross-module insights and AR aging, a first-run onboarding wizard, light/dark
theming, ~18 domain modals, and PWA installation with mobile navigation so an
approver can clear the queue from a phone.

### 1.4 The gap between the pitch and the build

This matters commercially, so it belongs in section 1 rather than buried:

- **"Agent-first" is aspirational.** 2 of 50 events reach an agent; 48 are audit
  and notification fan-out. One agent exists. The gap has *widened* as modules
  landed — leave and claims added a dozen event types and no consumer that acts.
- **The API is agent-*usable*, not agent-*discoverable*.** No OpenAPI document, no
  MCP server, no `.well-known` descriptor. `GET /v1/meta/departments` is the whole
  of the platform's self-description. An agent learns this API because a human
  pasted it into a prompt.
- **The built surface skews human.** One agent and two routed events against a
  console with a page per module, ~18 modals and a PWA manifest. A reader coming
  to the repo cold would call this *API-first software with a strong operator
  console* — which is a good thing to be, but it is not what the tagline says.

The repo says all three of these about itself in `docs/direction.md`. That honesty
is an asset; the mismatch is still a positioning problem.

**Also worth noting:** the last commit is 2026-08-02 — 17 days before this audit.
Four weeks of extremely high velocity followed by two and a half weeks of nothing.
For a portfolio review, that pattern is the thing to explain, not the code.

## 2. What is our USP?

### 2.1 The claimed USP vs. the defensible one

| Claimed | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Agent-first OS — AI agents are the users" | **Not yet true.** One agent, two routed events, no machine-readable API description. Aspiration, not differentiator. |
| "One API for the whole company" | **True and shipped**, but it is a *feature*, not a moat — Odoo and Zoho One say the same sentence. |
| "One database, not five integrated products" | **True, shipped, and genuinely differentiating.** This is the real USP. |
| "Built for Malaysia" | **True in HR, false in finance.** State-varying holidays yes; SST and e-invoicing no. Half-built localisation is the most dangerous kind. |

### 2.2 The USP as it actually stands, in priority order

**1. Cross-domain truth in one transaction — the only hard-to-copy thing here.**

An HR product can approve an expense claim. It cannot post the journal entry,
because it does not own your books. CompanyOS approves the claim and writes a
balanced, dimensioned double-entry posting in the same atomic batch — so the claim
lands in project margin and the cash-flow outlook in the same second, with no
export, no month-end reconciliation, and no integration to maintain. The same
property makes "which customers have open tickets *and* overdue invoices *and* an
open deal" a plain SQL join instead of a BI project.

This is real, it is shipped, and it is structurally unavailable to anyone selling
an integrated stack. It is also the hardest thing to *demo* in 30 seconds, which
is the marketing problem in §6.

**2. An autonomous agent with an audit trail and a deterministic floor.**

The collections agent is a real painkiller with a measurable number attached (days
sales outstanding). The engineering choice that makes it *sellable* rather than
scary is the fallback: LLM decisions are Zod-validated and every failure path
drops to a deterministic heuristic, so the process degrades instead of stopping,
and every decision — LLM or fallback — is in an append-only log you can show an
auditor. That combination is rarer in the market than the agent itself.

**3. Malaysian HR depth that is boring and therefore defensible.**

Holidays that vary by state, tenants running Sunday–Thursday weeks, Employment Act
minimums treated as a warning rather than a hard block, carry-forward that expires
on 31 March. Individually trivial; collectively a month of unglamorous work that
foreign entrants don't do and that an office manager notices on day one.

**4. A structural cost advantage.**

One Worker, one D1, R2, KV — the whole platform runs on Cloudflare's free plan
today, in queue-less inline mode, with upgrading being config-only. Marginal
infrastructure cost per tenant is cents; the only real variable cost is LLM tokens
for agent decisions. Against the abandoned Phase 0 plan (four OSS apps on a VPS
with adapters) this is a step change, and it means gross margin of 85–92% and the
freedom to price below anyone hosting containers per customer.

### 2.3 The one-sentence version I would actually use

> CompanyOS runs your finance, sales, support, projects and people on one database
> — so an approved expense claim moves your project margin instantly, and an AI
> agent chases your overdue invoices with the customer's whole history in front of
> it and an audit log behind it.

Note what that sentence does *not* lead with: "agent-first". Lead with the
outcome, keep the agent as the proof.

## 3. Who is the target user / customer?

### 3.1 The ICP the product has already chosen

Nobody wrote an ICP down, but the code has picked one. Reading it back:

- **MYR is the default base currency**; production runs on a `.com.my` domain.
- **Malaysian public holidays with state variation** ship as a data file.
- **Approvals resolve up the manager chain** because, as `docs/modules/approvals.md`
  says outright, *"a one-person finance function is the common Malaysian SME shape."*
- **Project dimensions on every journal line**, and the flagship rollup is project
  profitability — that is an agency/consultancy shape, not a retailer's.
- **JIRA/GitHub/Bitbucket ingestion** assumes an in-house engineering team.
- **Multi-company identity** assumes customers who run several entities.
- **No inventory anywhere** rules out trading and retail entirely.

### 3.2 The ICP, stated

**Primary: Malaysian project-based services businesses, 20–150 employees.**
Digital and creative agencies, software houses, engineering and IT consultancies,
professional services firms. Concretely, they look like this:

- Bills clients by project, retainer or milestone — so *project profitability* is a
  question they actually ask and currently answer in a spreadsheet.
- Has a one- or two-person finance function, an office manager doing HR, and no IT
  department.
- Currently runs SQL Account or AutoCount for books, a spreadsheet for leave, a
  WhatsApp group for claims, JIRA or Linear for work, and chases invoices by hand.
- Has an in-house dev team, so an API is an asset rather than an alien concept.
- English-speaking management, Klang Valley / Penang / Johor.
- **Buying trigger:** cash flow. Someone is spending Friday afternoons chasing
  payment and cannot say which clients are profitable.

**The economic buyer** is the founder/MD or the finance manager. **The champion**
is whoever is doing the chasing. **The blocker** is the external accountant or
auditor — and they know SQL and AutoCount and have never heard of you. Plan for
that person explicitly; they are the reason SME accounting migrations fail.

**Secondary: multi-entity SME groups (2–6 companies, shared back office).**
Multi-company identity with per-company email uniqueness is unusual in this price
band and genuinely painful without it. Small segment, low competition, high
willingness to pay.

**Tertiary (adjacent, not yet): the agency's own clients.** If an agency runs on
CompanyOS and likes it, it becomes a reseller. That is how vertical SaaS actually
distributes in this market — but it is a year-two motion, not a plan.

### 3.3 Who is explicitly *not* the customer

Naming these matters more than naming the ICP, because they are where the pipeline
will otherwise go to die:

- **Micro-businesses (761,897 firms — 70% of Malaysian MSMEs).** They need cheap
  compliant invoicing and nothing else. Bukku and the free MyInvois portal win;
  the product is far too heavy.
- **Retail, F&B, trading, distribution.** No inventory, no POS. Non-starter.
- **Anyone whose HR purchase is payroll-led.** Payroll, EPF/SOCSO/PCB submissions
  and statutory filing are deliberately out of scope. Kakitangan, Swingvy and
  BrioHR win that conversation before it starts.
- **Companies above ~250 employees.** Single-Worker, single-D1, no period locking,
  no fixed assets, no consolidation, no SSO.
- **Anyone needing multi-currency accounting today.** Rates are storable; ledger
  multi-currency is PRD-001c and unbuilt.
- **Anyone who needs e-invoicing this quarter — which is currently everyone.**
  See §6.2. This is the ICP-shaped hole in the middle of the ICP.

## 4. What is our TAM?

> **Constructed for this audit.** No market sizing exists in the repo. Firm counts are
> from DOSM's 2024 MSME release; pricing anchors are from published Malaysian market
> comparisons; every ratio applied to them is my estimate and labelled as such. Treat
> the shape as more reliable than any single figure. FX at ~RM4.4 = USD 1, approximate.

### 4.1 The market as counted

Malaysia had **1,086,386 MSMEs in 2024** — 96.1% of all business establishments —
split micro **761,897** (70.1%), small **306,764** (28.2%), medium **17,725**
(1.6%). Services is **84.4%** of them (917,070 firms). MSMEs produced RM652.4bn in
value added, 39.5% of GDP.

### 4.2 Bottom-up sizing

**Step 1 — the band that can use this at all.** Small + medium = **324,489**
firms. Micro is out (§3.3).

**Step 2 — services only.** ×84.4% ≈ **274,000** firms. This is the theoretical
ceiling if CompanyOS were feature-complete for every service business.

**Step 3 — project-based services, the real ICP.** Agencies, consultancies,
software houses, professional services — as against the retail, logistics,
healthcare, education and F&B that dominate the services count. *My estimate:
15–20% of services SMEs* → **41,000–55,000 firms; call it 48,000.**

**Step 4 — annual contract value.** Published anchors: Malaysian SME cloud
accounting runs RM20–200/month, a 5-user SME budgets RM500–800/month all-in, and
SQL/AutoCount perpetual licences are RM1,500–3,000 plus annual support. An
all-in-one replacing accounting + CRM + helpdesk + PM + HR should command a
multiple of the accounting-only line, but SME price sensitivity is brutal.
*My model:* Starter (≤20 staff) RM499/mo, Growth (21–75) RM1,299/mo, Scale
(76–200) RM2,999/mo → **blended mature ACV RM15,600/year**, with early
design-partner ACV nearer RM9,600.

### 4.3 The layers

| Layer | Definition | Firms | Annual value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** — theoretical | All Malaysian small+medium services SMEs, if feature-complete | ~274,000 | **~RM4.3bn** (~USD 975m) |
| **TAM** — ICP-bounded | Project-based services SMEs, 20–150 staff | ~48,000 | **~RM750m** (~USD 170m) |
| **SAM** — reachable | Klang Valley + Penang + Johor, English-speaking, digitally mature, no inventory or payroll dependency, willing to move books (~15% of ICP) | ~7,200 | **~RM112m** (~USD 25m) |
| **SOM** — 3-year obtainable | Founder-led sales, one market, credible execution (0.5–1.5% of SAM) | **36–108** | **RM0.6m–1.7m ARR** |

### 4.4 What the numbers actually say

**TAM is not the constraint and never will be.** A RM750m ICP-bounded TAM
comfortably supports a RM20–50m ARR business; the gap between RM112m SAM and
RM1.7m SOM is entirely execution, trust and distribution. Anyone presenting the
RM4.3bn figure to an investor is presenting the wrong number — and the SOM row is
the only one that should drive a decision.

**Regional expansion multiplies TAM and multiplies cost in the same motion.**
Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines together are perhaps
5–10× Malaysia's SME count, but the Malaysian localisation that is an asset in §2
is a *boundary*: each new market needs its own holiday calendar, work-week norms,
statutory floors, tax regime and e-invoicing integration. Budget one engineer-quarter
per market minimum, and do not treat ASEAN as one market. Singapore is the
cheapest second market (English, IRAS InvoiceNow, similar company shapes) and the
right one to model, not Indonesia.

**The most useful sizing question is not TAM.** It is: *how many project-based
Malaysian services firms of 20–150 people are within two introductions of you?*
If that number is 30, you have a business to start. If it is 3, distribution is
the whole project and §6 is the only section that matters.

## 5. Revenue projection — three years

> **Constructed for this audit.** There are no customers, no pricing page, no signed
> LOIs and no billing system, so this is a model, not a forecast. Its purpose is to make
> the assumptions arguable. Years run Sep→Aug from 2026.

### 5.1 Three preconditions the model assumes

None of these are true today, and the model is void without them:

1. **SST + MyInvois shipped** (PRD-001b plus new e-invoicing work). Without it
   there is no legal invoicing product in Malaysia. Assumed live by Q2 FY1.
2. **A billing system exists.** There is currently no plan, seat, subscription or
   metering concept anywhere in the product — the `tenants` table has four columns.
   Manual invoicing works for the first ten customers and nothing beyond.
3. **Someone is selling full-time.** At founder-led SME ACVs, revenue is a
   function of sales hours, not of features.

### 5.2 Base case

| | FY1 (Sep 26–Aug 27) | FY2 | FY3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design partners (free) | 3 | — | — |
| Paying customers, end of year | **10** | **38** | **93** |
| Net adds | 10 | 28 | 55 |
| Blended ACV | RM9,600 | RM12,000 | RM15,600 |
| **Exit ARR** | **RM96k** | **RM456k** | **RM1.45m** |
| Recognised subscription revenue | RM32k | RM240k | RM830k |
| Implementation & migration services | RM18k | RM70k | RM120k |
| **Total recognised revenue** | **RM50k** | **RM310k** | **RM950k** |
| Gross margin | ~80% | ~86% | ~89% |

Drivers: FY1 is three unpaid design partners converting late, then founder-sold
referrals — first paid customer Q3 at the earliest, because compliance work eats
H1. FY2 adds an accountant/reseller channel and ACV rises as SST, credit notes and
the sales agent land. FY3 compounds referral plus channel with a second seller.
Services revenue is deliberately material: this market expects data migration off
SQL/AutoCount to be a paid engagement, and pretending otherwise loses deals.

### 5.3 Scenarios

| Scenario | FY1 | FY2 | FY3 | FY3 exit ARR | What makes it happen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** | RM12k | RM90k | RM320k | RM520k | Compliance work slips, project stays part-time, no channel, sold one at a time by the founder between other work. This is the default outcome of the current 17-day-idle trajectory. |
| **Base** | RM50k | RM310k | RM950k | RM1.45m | The three preconditions land, ten design partners come from the existing network, one accountant channel partner produces referrals, one dedicated seller from FY2. |
| **Bull** | RM120k | RM850k | RM2.8m | RM4.2m | MyInvois + SST ship by Q1 FY1 and are marketed *as the migration path*, 20+ accounting-firm channel partners, two sellers from FY2, payroll added in FY2 to unlock the HR wedge, Singapore opened in FY3. Needs real capital. |

### 5.4 Unit economics as modelled

- **CAC:** RM4,000–8,000 founder-led (time-costed); RM10,000–15,000 with paid
  acquisition, which I would not recommend in this segment.
- **Payback:** 6–10 months at the Growth tier. Healthy.
- **Gross churn:** budget 2–3%/month early, improving as the ledger makes switching
  costly. An accounting system that holds your books has structurally low churn
  once it holds them — *and* is structurally very hard to get adopted in the first
  place. Those are the same fact seen from both sides.
- **Variable cost:** LLM tokens, ~RM20–60/tenant/month at modelled agent volumes.
  Meter this per tenant before pricing hard; today nothing counts it.
- **Infrastructure:** effectively rounding error. This is the structural advantage.

### 5.5 What would most change these numbers

In order of leverage:

1. **The compliance gap.** It is a binary gate on all three scenarios, and it is
   also — if shipped early — the single best marketing hook available (§6.2).
2. **Channel.** One productive accounting firm with 200 SME clients is worth more
   than any amount of content marketing. It is also the hardest thing to get,
   because that firm's revenue is tied to the software it already resells.
3. **Payroll.** Would roughly double ACV and unlock a second wedge, and is
   currently rejected on strategy grounds. That rejection is defensible as
   *moat* reasoning and expensive as *revenue* reasoning. Worth re-litigating.
4. **Whether anyone sells full-time.** Nothing else on this list matters if not.

## 6. How hard would this be to market, distribute and sell?

**Short answer: hard — the hardest part of the whole venture, and considerably
harder than building it was.** Scores below are difficulty, 10 = hardest.

### 6.1 Marketing — 6/10

**Against you.** The product is five categories at once, so there is no natural
search term to rank for and no obvious comparison page to write; "business
operating system" is a phrase buyers do not type. The real USP — one database, so
a claim moves project margin — takes two sentences and a diagram to land, which is
one sentence more than a cold prospect grants you. "Agent-first" is a strong hook
that the build cannot yet substantiate, and over-claiming it to a technical SME
audience is a credibility risk, not just a stretch.

**For you.** The AI-agent narrative is genuinely timely and this is a real working
agent, not a chatbot wrapper. The collections story has a number attached (DSO),
and numbers market themselves. The claim→GL→margin demo is the kind of thing that
makes a finance person sit up, because they know what it costs them today. Malaysia
is small enough that founder-led LinkedIn plus two or three industry
associations reach most of the ICP — and the brand pack and positioning copy are
already written.

**Do this:** market the collections agent, not the OS. One painkiller, one number,
one 90-second demo. The platform story is what you tell them *after* they are a
customer.

### 6.2 Distribution — 8/10, and one blocker that outranks everything

**The blocker.** Malaysia's LHDN e-invoicing mandate now reaches businesses above
**RM500k** annual turnover (RM1m–5m from 1 January 2026, RM500k–1m from 1 July
2026, with relaxation periods and penalties of RM200–20,000 per non-compliant
invoice thereafter). Every firm in the §3 ICP is inside that net *today*.
CompanyOS's flagship module issues invoices, and it has **no MyInvois integration
at all** — the term does not appear anywhere in the repo — and **no SST in the
ledger**, which `docs/prd/PRD-001-finance-ledger-completeness.md` already
identifies as blocking Malaysian commercial use. Until both exist, the product
cannot legally be a customer's invoicing system, and no amount of GTM effort
routes around that.

**The same fact is the biggest opportunity in the plan.** The mandate is forcing
every SME in the country to open up and change its invoicing stack in 2026 — a
compelled evaluation event across the entire ICP, which is the best migration
window this market will see in a decade. Incumbents are using it as their headline
(SQL Account markets LHDN, SST and e-invoice as built-in). Arriving late to your
own market's forced migration is the most expensive timing error available here.

**The rest of the distribution problem.** Malaysian SME back-office software sells
through **dealers, accountants and company secretaries**, not through self-serve
signups: AutoCount serves 240,000+ companies and SQL 600,000+ users, both through
established reseller networks that earn on licences and implementation. You cannot
outspend that and you should not try to bypass it — but recruiting those partners
means asking them to sell against the product paying their bills. Expect a long
courtship with a small number of them, and expect the first productive one to come
from a personal relationship.

**Realistic channels, ranked:** (1) founder's own network — agencies you have
worked with; (2) two or three accounting or company-secretarial firms serving
services SMEs; (3) industry associations and agency communities; (4) developer-led
inbound off the API and the agent story — cheap, slow, and the only one that
scales without headcount. Self-serve product-led growth is not available to
software that holds a general ledger.

### 6.3 Sales — 8/10

**The core difficulty is trust, not product.** You are asking an SME to move its
**books** — the single highest-consequence system it owns — to an unknown vendor
with no customers, no auditor familiarity and no local brand. The finance manager
does not decide this; the external accountant does, and that accountant has run
SQL or AutoCount for fifteen years and carries professional risk if it goes wrong.
Add the migration itself (chart of accounts, opening balances, historical
invoices, receipts) and the honest sales cycle is **3–9 months**, with a
proof-of-concept phase, at an ACV that does not fund a field sales team.

**And the replacement ask is five-headed.** Every prospect has an incumbent in each
of finance, CRM, support, PM and HR, each with a champion who chose it. "Replace
all five" is the hardest proposition in SME software; the all-in-one story that
looks like the strength is what makes the deal enormous, and enormous deals do not
close for a vendor with zero references.

**What genuinely helps.** Land-and-expand is available and cheap here: the leave +
claims module is a **low-trust, high-frequency** purchase — nobody's books are at
risk, every employee touches it weekly, and it drags the ledger in behind it via
claim postings when trust exists. So does the collections wedge: run the agent
alongside their existing accounting system and let it earn a number before you ask
for the general ledger. Both convert an impossible replacement sale into a
survivable one. Aim your first ten conversations at people who already trust you —
design partners, not prospects.

### 6.4 Summary

| Dimension | Difficulty | The one thing that decides it |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | 6/10 | Whether you lead with the collections painkiller instead of the OS |
| Distribution | 8/10 | Whether MyInvois + SST ship in time to ride the mandate |
| Sales | 8/10 | Whether you enter through leave/claims or collections rather than the ledger |
| **Overall GTM** | **8/10** | Distribution and trust. The code is not the bottleneck and has not been for a while. |

## 7. Gaps that stand between this and revenue

Ranked by commercial consequence, not by effort.

| # | Gap | Consequence | Status in repo |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **MyInvois / LHDN e-invoicing** | Cannot legally be a Malaysian customer's invoicing system in 2026 | **Not mentioned anywhere** |
| 2 | **SST end to end in the ledger** | Same, plus no derivable tax position | PRD-001b written, S12 **not started**, explicitly deferred "until a design partner hits them" |
| 3 | **Billing / subscription / metering** | No way to charge a customer, or to know what a tenant costs | Absent from the product entirely |
| 4 | **Payroll** | HR cannot be the wedge; ACV capped | Deliberately rejected in `docs/prd/README.md` |
| 5 | **Agent guardrails + eval (PRD-002)** | Blocks the SalesAgent; also the thing that makes "autonomous" sellable to a cautious buyer | S10 **not started** — and it gates S15 |
| 6 | **Credit notes, ledger multi-currency** | Ordinary finance operations that will fail a diligence review | PRD-001c, S13 **not started** |

Note the pattern: **items 1, 2, 3 and 9 are all cheap relative to what has already
been built, and all four are load-bearing for revenue.** Meanwhile the unbuilt
backlog (S9–S15) is mostly more product. The portfolio is over-invested in
capability and under-invested in sellability.

## 8. Recommended next 90 days

Opinionated, and deliberately excludes new modules.

**Weeks 1–2 — validate before building.** Ten conversations with people in the ICP
you already know. One question above all: *what are you doing about e-invoicing?*
Their answer sizes the wedge and tells you whether §4's ratios survive. Do not
demo; ask.

**Weeks 1–6 — close the compliance gate (parallel).** SST end to end per PRD-001b,
then MyInvois submission. This is the gate on every scenario in §5 and the best
marketing hook you have. Ship it while the mandate's relaxation period is still
running and it is a migration path; ship it in 2027 and it is table stakes you
lack.

**Weeks 4–8 — make it chargeable.** A plan/seat/subscription concept, per-tenant
LLM cost metering, and a pricing page. Three tiers as modelled in §4.2. This is
days of work and it currently caps revenue at zero.

**Weeks 6–12 — three design partners, free, in writing.** Chosen for the collections
wedge: project-based services firms with a real AR problem. Requirement: they run
the agent against live invoices and you get to publish the DSO number. One
published number from one named Malaysian company is worth more than the entire
S9–S15 backlog.

**Weeks 8–12 — the credibility artefacts.** An OpenAPI document and an MCP server
(days, not weeks) so "agent-first" has something behind it. PRD-002's guardrails
and kill switch before any agent messages a stranger — that is a prerequisite for
selling autonomy, not just for S15.

**Explicitly defer:** S9 quote signing, S11 support intake, S14 project
scheduling, S15 SalesAgent, payroll. All are good work. None of them is why there
is no revenue.

**And decide the thing this audit cannot decide for you:** whether CompanyOS is a
product to sell or the operating system for *your own* companies. The two answers
imply completely different next 90 days — the second one makes §§4–6 irrelevant
and makes the current build state close to sufficient. The repo reads like the
first; the 17-day pause reads like the second.
